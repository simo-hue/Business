# Regulift: where to launch first, and in which order to expand

Prepared 2026-10-18 for the EIT Community AI Entrepreneurs Lab application and later investor talks. All sources were accessed on 2026-10-18. Figures are quoted from the source with their location. Where a figure comes only from a search snippet or a secondary report, the grade says so.

Quality grades, the same scale as in number-claims-sources.md:

- **A**: statute, official gazette, court decision, statistical office, EU institution or agency, or peer-reviewed research.
- **B**: named analyst or large practitioner dataset with a described method.
- **C**: vendor or practitioner survey with self-reported or modelled figures.
- **D**: blog, search snippet, secondary report, or our own estimate.
- **U**: not verified in this session, stated from background knowledge. Do not put U items on a slide.

## 1. One-page summary

**Recommendation: launch in Austria, expand to Germany second and Italy third.** Use Hungary as a home base for design partners, not as a revenue market. Keep the Netherlands as the best English-language option if a fourth market is needed.

**Why Austria first.**

- **The trigger is live right now.** Austria's NIS2 law, the NISG 2026, has applied since 1 October 2026. Entities must register within three months, so by about the end of December 2026. Austria is the freshest NIS2 window among all candidates. In most other countries the law has been in force for a year or more and registration has closed.
- **The founders can be there in person.** One founder is Austrian. Vienna is a direct train of 2 h 48 from Budapest, every hour. Customers and law firms can be met face to face every week.
- **The market is small but dense.** Austria has 7,890 enterprises with 50 or more staff, or 861 per million inhabitants. That is above the EU average of 680 and close to Germany's 959.
- **Lawyers are expensive, so the savings pitch is strong.** The official Austrian tariff works out to about €349 an hour at the bar's default value. Legal-services turnover is €441 per inhabitant, against €100 in Hungary.
- **It is a training ground for Germany.** Austria's rules on legal advice are strict, and so are Germany's. A partner-law-firm model that works under Austrian rules will work in Germany with small changes. The language, and much of the client base, is shared.
- **There is public pre-seed money.** The aws Preseed Innovative Solutions grant pays up to €89,000, or €100,000 with the gender bonus. It is open before incorporation.

**Why Germany second.** It is the largest market by far, with 80,043 enterprises with 50 or more staff. Its NIS2 law has been in force since 6 December 2025, and the BSI expects about 29,500 regulated entities. Germany has a national AI Act law that names the Bundesnetzagentur. It comes second, not first, for three reasons. Registration closed in spring 2026, so the first wave of urgent buyers has already acted. The founders have no base there. And the German compliance-software field is crowded: DataGuard, heyData, Secjur and Kertos are all German, according to our own market scan (grade D).

**Why Italy third.** Italy is large, with 31,851 enterprises with 50 or more staff, 11,209 of them in manufacturing. It has a live NIS2 law, and security measures fall due in October 2026. It also has its own national AI law, Law 132/2025. Its rules on legal advice are the most open of the top candidates, because out-of-court advice is reserved to lawyers only where it is linked to litigation. The Italian founder can sell there, and five Italian universities belong to the 28DIGITAL (formerly EIT Digital) Master School. Italy comes third because lawyers are cheaper, so the pitch must rest on time and evidence rather than on the legal bill. Italy is also far from Budapest without flying.

**What the scoring table says, honestly.** Germany and Italy score highest on the seven criteria when every criterion counts the same, with Austria in fifth place. Austria wins as the first market on the practical test the team set: a market the founders can reach in person, with a trigger that is live this quarter, beats a bigger market they cannot reach. This is a judgement, not a result of the scores.

**Where the research is thin.**

- Eurostat splits company size only into 50 to 249 and 250 or more. The 50 to 1,000 band cannot be isolated, and the 250 or more class includes very large firms.
- No source counts firms that "sell into regulated supply chains". Manufacturing and IT services are used as proxies.
- There is no current market survey of business-lawyer hourly rates for Austria, Italy, France, Hungary or the CEE countries. The Austrian figure is a tariff calculation. The cross-country bands date from 2007.
- No law firm or bar publishes a fixed price for GDPR, NIS2 or AI Act readiness work in any candidate country.
- Austria has not yet designated an AI Act market surveillance authority, and Hungary's designation could not be found.
- The rules on legal advice for Switzerland, Belgium, the Nordics, Poland and Romania were not checked against the statutes in this session.
- The research agents ran out of their search budget partway through. Gaps are listed in each section.

How each score was set:

1. **Company base.** Enterprises with 50 or more persons employed, 2024, and density per million inhabitants (table in section 3).
2. **NIS2 live.** 5 means the law is in force and the registration or audit trigger is happening now. 1 means no law.
3. **AI Act and DORA setup.** 5 means a national AI Act law and a designated authority. Lower means designation pending or not found. Switzerland is outside both laws.
4. **Legal prices.** Based on hourly-rate anchors and legal-services turnover per inhabitant. Higher prices mean more savings per customer.
5. **Legal-advice rules.** Based on statutes and case law (section 5 and the country sections). Scores for Switzerland, Belgium, the Nordics and Poland rest on unverified background knowledge (U).
6. **Partners and ecosystem.** Lawyers per inhabitant, 28DIGITAL presence, public startup support, the European Innovation Scoreboard, venture capital per GDP, and legal tech counts.
7. **Founder presence and language.** The founders sell in German, Italian, English and Hungarian, and live between Austria and Budapest.

## 4. Country sections

### 4.1 Austria (recommended first market)

- **Company base.** 6,367 enterprises with 50 to 249 staff and 1,523 with 250 or more, or 861 per million inhabitants. Manufacturing accounts for 1,895 of them and IT services for 284. A secondary source puts NISG 2026 coverage at about 4,000 companies in 18 sectors (Blck Alpaca, July 2026, D). Do not put this figure on a slide.
- **NIS2.** The NISG 2026 has applied since 1 October 2026 and replaces the NISG 2018. Registration is due within three months of entry into force. Regulated entities must manage supply-chain security, so their suppliers will get questionnaires from late 2026. This is the strongest timing argument in the whole comparison.
- **AI Act and DORA.** No AI Act authority is designated and there is no implementing law. The FMA supervises DORA under the DORA-Vollzugsgesetz. Vienna's banks and insurers create indirect DORA demand among their IT suppliers.
- **Prices.** The tariff anchor is about €349 an hour. Legal-services turnover is €441 per inhabitant, in the top group. No market rate survey exists.
- **Legal advice.** Strict. See section 5.
- **Ecosystem.** The aws Preseed Innovative Solutions grant pays "EUR 89.000", up to €100,000 with the gender bonus, and covers 80% of costs. It is open before incorporation or "bis maximal 6 Monate nach Eintragung ins Firmenbuch" (https://www.aws.at/aws-preseed-innovative-solutions/, A). The aws Seedfinancing grant goes up to €356,000 (A). The FFG invests "rund 90 Millionen Euro" a year in startup projects (https://www.ffg.at/startups, A). The Austrian Startup Monitor 2025 counts 3,688 startups since 2014. Of these, 64% are B2B, 47% were founded in Vienna, and 60% have received public funding (AIT, AustrianStartups and WU, April 2026, B). There is no 28DIGITAL office in Austria, and venture capital per GDP was low in 2025.
- **Founders.** One founder is Austrian, and German is a sales language. Vienna is under three hours from Budapest.
- **Weaknesses.** The market is small, so Austria alone will not carry an investor story. The country has no AI Act authority yet, and the rules on legal advice are strict.

### 4.2 Germany (recommended second market)

- **Company base.** 80,043 enterprises with 50 or more staff, the largest of all candidates. 18,432 are in manufacturing and 2,827 in IT services.
- **NIS2.** The NIS2UmsuCG has been in force since 6 December 2025. The BSI expects about 29,500 entities. The registration deadline was 6 March 2026. The government estimates an average of 2,752 hours and €60,700 in material costs per particularly important entity per year (Drucksache 21/1501, A).
- **AI Act and DORA.** The KI-MIG names the Bundesnetzagentur as market surveillance authority, in force since 29 July 2026. BaFin supervises DORA.
- **Prices.** The average standard rate is €244 an hour (BRAK STAR 2024, B). Large-firm rates are higher, but no current method-based figure was found.
- **Legal advice.** Middle. RDG § 2(1) defines a legal service as any activity in another person's concrete matter that "eine rechtliche Prüfung des Einzelfalls erfordert". Under RDG § 3 such services are "unzulässig, soweit sie nicht erlaubt wird". In Smartlaw (BGH, 9 Sep 2021, I ZR 113/20) the court held that a document generator "wird nicht in einer konkreten Angelegenheit des Nutzers tätig", "ähnlich wie bei einem Formularhandbuch". Regulift analyses one company's real systems, so Smartlaw does not cover company-specific legal conclusions. BRAO § 49b(3) bans any share of fees "für die Vermittlung von Aufträgen". Sources: gesetze-im-internet.de (A) and BGH press release 171/2021 (A).
- **Correction to the strategy brief.** OLG Hamm, 12 May 2026, 4 UKl 3/25, is an unfair-competition case about a clinic chatbot, not a case under the RDG. No court has yet ruled on AI legal advice under the RDG (Remmertz on legal-tech-verzeichnis.de, 10 Jun 2026, B).
- **Ecosystem.** The EXIST stipend pays graduates €2,500 a month for 12 months (https://www.ptj.de/foerdermoeglichkeiten/exist/gruendungsstipendium, A). It needs a German university host. Germany has the most legal tech companies of all candidates (149 in CodeX) and the most competing compliance vendors.
- **Why second.** The same language, and the same legal logic as Austria. Austrian reference customers and partner firms carry over. Munich is the natural first German city from Vienna.

### 4.3 Italy (recommended third market)

- **Company base.** 31,851 enterprises with 50 or more staff. 11,209 are in manufacturing, the second-highest count after Germany. The industrial supply chains of the north fit the NIS2 supply-chain route.
- **NIS2.** D.Lgs. 138/2024 has been in force since 16 October 2024. ACN runs a yearly registration from 1 January to 28 February. Incident reporting has been mandatory since January 2026, and security measures fall due in October 2026.
- **AI Act.** Law 132/2025 is a national AI law. ACN is the market surveillance authority and AgID the notifying authority. Article 13 lets professionals use AI only for "attività strumentali e di supporto", "con prevalenza del lavoro intellettuale", and they must tell clients which AI systems they use (https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:2025-09-23;132~art13, A). Partner lawyers using Regulift will have to disclose it to their clients.
- **Prices.** The only hourly figure is the 2007 estimate of €180 to €249. Legal-services turnover is €356 per inhabitant, but spread over 182,616 enterprises, which suggests many small practices.
- **Legal advice.** The most open of the top candidates. Law 247/2012, art. 2(6), reserves out-of-court advice to lawyers only "ove connessa all'attività giurisdizionale, se svolta in modo continuativo, sistematico e organizzato" (normattiva.it, A). The criminal courts read "connected to litigation" broadly, as activity "destinata a incidere su un contenzioso ... presente o futuro" (Cass. pen. VI n. 18734/2025, reported by misterlex.it, D). Outsiders may hold up to one third of a lawyers' company under art. 4-bis (A). The professional code bans commissions to third parties for introducing clients (art. 37, D).
- **Ecosystem.** 28DIGITAL has offices in Trento and Bolzano and five partner universities, more than any other candidate. Italy is a RIS country. Smart&Start Italia offers a "Finanziamento a tasso zero dell'80% delle spese ammissibili", or 90% for teams that are all women or all under 36 (https://www.invitalia.it/incentivi-e-strumenti/smartstart-italia, A). This needs an Italian company.
- **Why third.** Italy is a large market with a native-speaking founder and lighter rules on legal advice. But lawyers are cheaper, and the founders cannot reach it as easily as Vienna. Start in the north: Milan, Trento and Bolzano, where Bolzano also works in German.

### 4.4 Hungary (home base for design partners)

- **Company base.** 5,855 enterprises with 50 or more staff, of which 2,019 are in manufacturing.
- **NIS2.** Act LXIX of 2024, in force since 1 January 2025, adds a requirement that is not in the directive. Every entity must contract an auditor registered with SZTFH within 120 days of registration and be audited every two years. Entities that already operated before 2025 needed their first audit by 30 June 2026. This creates recurring evidence demand, which is exactly what an evidence pack serves.
- **AI Act.** No authority is listed and no decree was found.
- **Prices.** Legal-services turnover is €956m, or €100 per inhabitant, the lowest of the usable figures. A pitch about savings on the legal bill is weak here.
- **Legal advice.** The strictest of the founders' markets. Act LXXVIII of 2017, § 2(1)(c) and § 4(1), reserves "jogi tanácsadás" (legal advice) done "rendszeresen és ellenérték fejében" (regularly and for consideration) to attorneys. Criminal Code § 286 punishes unauthorised attorney activity done as a business ("zugírászat") with up to two years in prison. However, § 3(1)(p) lets attorneys develop and sell legal IT systems, so a Hungarian law firm could resell Regulift (https://njt.hu/jogszabaly/2017-78-00-00, A).
- **Why not first.** The market is small, the legal prices are low, and the legal-advice risk is criminal. Use Budapest, the 28DIGITAL office and ELTE for design partners and pilots that test the evidence pack against the SZTFH audit. Do not plan revenue here first.

### 4.5 Netherlands (best English-language alternative)

- **Company base.** 13,782 enterprises with 50 or more staff and a high density of IT firms, 41 per million inhabitants.
- **NIS2.** The Cyberbeveiligingswet has applied since 15 August 2026, which gives a fresh window similar to Austria's.
- **Legal advice.** The Advocatenwet protects only the title: "Tot het voeren van de titel van advocaat is uitsluitend gerechtigd ..." (art. 9a, https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0002093/2026-01-01, A). No monopoly on advice was found (U).
- **Ecosystem.** It is an innovation leader, has the Eindhoven office, and hosts the last two days of the AI Lab in Amsterdam.
- **Weakness.** The founders do not speak Dutch, and they have no presence there. Buyers in the 50 to 249 band often expect Dutch. This is our judgement, not measured.

### 4.6 Short notes on the rest

- **Switzerland.** High prices, open rules on legal advice, and German-speaking. But it is not in the EU, it has no NIS2 supply-chain duty and no AI Act, and it is not eligible for the AI Lab. It is a later add-on for German-speaking customers, not a launch market.
- **France.** It is large, but it has no NIS2 law yet, Article 54 of the Law of 31 December 1971 is strict, and the founders do not sell in French. Postpone it.
- **Nordics.** Innovation leaders with high venture capital and open rules on legal advice (U). Sweden's and Finland's laws are live. The founders have no language or presence there.
- **Belgium and Luxembourg.** Belgium has a live law and the CyberFundamentals framework, with a 2027 deadline for the "Essential" level (D). Luxembourg is tiny and its rules on legal advice are strict.
- **Czechia, Poland, Romania.** Legal prices are low. Czechia also bans brokering legal services by unauthorised persons (Act 85/1996, § 2(3), A). Poland's AI Act law is new and its NIS2 fines reportedly start in 2028 (D).

## 5. How the partner-law-firm model must be structured in Austria

### The rules

- **Advice is reserved to lawyers, and so is information.** RAO § 8(1) gives lawyers the "Befugnis zur berufsmäßigen Parteienvertretung in allen gerichtlichen und außergerichtlichen, in allen öffentlichen und privaten Angelegenheiten". § 8(2) reserves this to lawyers (https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=10001673&Paragraf=8, A). The Vienna Administrative Court held that the reserved activity includes "das Beratungsrecht", and that even "die Erteilung von Rechtsauskünften" suffices. It fined an online claims platform (VwG Wien, 5 Feb 2024, VGW-001/049/7870/2023, A).
- **The penalty is administrative.** Under RAO § 57(2), offering or performing a reserved activity as a business without authority is fined up to €16,000 (A). Commercial drafting of submissions for courts or authorities ("Winkelschreiberei") is a separate offence under EGVG Art. III(1) Z 1 (A).
- **Consultants get no general legal-advice right.** Under GewO § 136(3), business consultants have no general right to give legal advice (A). Ancillary services are capped at 30% of turnover under GewO § 32(1a) (A). The WKO says a non-lawyer may act as data protection officer, with legal tasks only as an ancillary part (WKO guidance, 30 Jan 2018, B).
- **No commissions and no fee collection.** In OGH 4 Ob 77/23m of 27 Jun 2023, the Supreme Court prohibited a platform from taking "einen Prozentsatz des vereinnahmten Honorars von Rechtsanwälten" for brokering lawyers, even with software bundled in. It also prohibited collecting the client's fees and forwarding them minus a commission. The rule it applied is RL-BA 2015 § 47(3) Z 6, which bans "Provisionen, also Anteile seines Honorars". A platform fee must match "konkrete ... erbrachte Leistungen". The court dismissed the claim to ban recommending lawyers "maschinell oder durch Verwendung von Künstlicher Intelligenz", so matching clients to lawyers by machine is not prohibited (https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/Justiz/JJT_20230627_OGH0002_0040OB00077_23M0000_000/JJT_20230627_OGH0002_0040OB00077_23M0000_000.html, A).
- **No outside ownership or profit share.** RAO § 21c, in force since 1 Aug 2026 (BGBl. I 63/2026), allows only lawyers and former lawyers as shareholders. "Andere Personen als Gesellschafter dürfen am Umsatz oder Gewinn ... nicht beteiligt sein" (A).

### The structure that follows

1. **Regulift's own output stays factual.** It covers the context report, the inventory, the evidence, and "questions for legal review", each tied to the article it concerns. It must not tell the customer "you are in scope of NIS2" or "you comply with Article 32 GDPR". Austria has no Smartlaw-style ruling, and the Vienna court treats even legal information as reserved. So deterministic threshold checks should also be worded as facts, for example "headcount 120, sector Annex I no. 8", with the conclusion signed by a lawyer.
2. **The lawyer gives every legal conclusion.** The law firm signs gap ratings, applicability verdicts and remediation advice in the shared workspace. They go out under the lawyer's name and professional liability. The workspace should show which statements are signed, by whom, and when.
3. **The client contracts the law firm directly.** The mandate and the invoice run between the customer and the firm. Regulift does not collect lawyers' fees and does not forward them.
4. **Regulift's revenue never depends on mandates.** The customer pays a SaaS subscription. A partner firm may pay a fixed fee for a workspace licence, priced for concrete services such as seats, storage and integrations. No percentage of fees, no fee per mandate, and no success fee.
5. **Matching is allowed, commission is not.** Regulift may recommend partner firms, including by algorithm, because the OGH dismissed that claim. It must not be paid for the recommendation itself.
6. **No equity link with law firms.** Regulift cannot hold shares in a partner firm or share in its profits. Whether partner lawyers may invest in Regulift was not researched. Check this with the Austrian Bar (ÖRAK) before any investor round that includes lawyers.
7. **Careful marketing.** Avoid words such as "rechtssicher" (legally certain) or "legal check" for Regulift's own output. They suggest legal advice and invite unfair-competition claims.
8. **Optional data protection officer service.** A non-lawyer data protection officer is possible in Austria. If Regulift adds such a service later, the legal tasks in it must stay ancillary or go to the partner firm.

The same structure works in Germany, which bans fee sharing under BRAO § 49b(3) and outside profit shares under § 59i. In Italy the same structure is also safe. There, the partner firm must also tell clients that it uses AI, under art. 13 of Law 132/2025. In Hungary the partner firm can be the reseller under Act LXXVIII of 2017, § 3(1)(p).

Have an Austrian lawyer review this structure before launch. This section is research, not legal advice.
