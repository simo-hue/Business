Regulift product proposal
Regulift. Product proposal.18 October 2026, for Simone and Andreas

One product, built to be legal in the strictest market first

Regulift reads a company's systems from the inside, collects the evidence, helps the company fix what is missing, and brings a partner law firm into the same workspace to confirm compliance in writing. This page says, in plain words, what the product does, which parts are legal everywhere, which parts need a lawyer's signature in strict countries, and what we must never do.

1. What the product doesFive steps, each with its legal status

From a read-only connection to a signed confirmation

Step 1

Connect

The company connects repositories, cloud accounts, HR and CRM tools with read-only access. Nothing is changed, nothing is copied out.

Legal
Step 2

Report and approve

The collector writes a report about what the company runs and where data flows. No records, no file contents. One named person at the company approves it. Only then does it reach Regulift.

Legal
Step 3

Inventory and evidence

Regulift builds the inventory, the data-flow map and the evidence record, and compares it against a checklist the partner law firm wrote and published under its name.

Legal
Step 4

Improve, in loops

The company fixes gaps and uploads evidence. Technical fixes and template documents come from Regulift at once. Anything that judges the company's specific case is drafted by the AI and released by the lawyer.

Legal Lawyer signs
Step 5

Confirm

The partner lawyer opens the same workspace, reviews the evidence pack and confirms compliance in writing, under the firm's name. The company pays the firm directly for that.

Lawyer signs

The software finds and organises the facts. Only a lawyer gives the verdict. That one sentence is the whole legal design.

2. Feature by feature, market by marketSame code everywhere, one setting per country

What the customer sees without a lawyer, and where

Countries disagree about one thing: who may tell a company "this law applies to you" or "you comply". So every feature has a status per market group. Closed markets reserve legal advice to lawyers and have no safe harbour for software. Germany is strict but its highest court protects generic, rule-based tools. 3. The improvement loopFeedback without advice

Customers iterate until they are ready. Most feedback is not legal advice.

Every upload needs an answer within seconds, not after a lawyer has looked. That works because the loop has four layers, and only the last one is reserved to lawyers in strict countries.

Technical status Legal

Engineering feedback against a checklist. Control platforms show this to German and Austrian customers today without lawyers. It is the bulk of NIS2, CRA and GDPR security work.

Mechanical comparison Legal

"Evidence present or missing for item 14." The checklist and its acceptance criteria are the firm's. Regulift matches and flips the status as the customer fixes things. Progress reads "38 of 52 items", never "compliant".

Generic guidance and templates Legal

The AI explains the rule in general and fills the firm's templates with the company's facts. A form generator working from the firm's forms. In Austria, show it as the firm's material.

Individual judgement Lawyer signs

"Your scoring model is high-risk." "You now comply." The AI drafts, the lawyer releases. Needed at the start (scoping), at the end (confirmation), and for exceptions in between.

How we keep it fast

  • Framework mandate at onboarding. The company signs a direct engagement with the partner firm on day one, at a flat or capped fee the firm sets and bills. Every grey-zone question in the loop is then covered, and the lawyer answers inside the workspace within an agreed time.
  • Two lawyer touchpoints by design. Scoping at the start, confirmation at the end. Everything between is self-service against the firm's checklist, with an "ask the lawyer" button.
  • The AI drafts everything the lawyer signs. The lawyer reviews instead of writing from a blank page. That is where the hours drop, and where the 60% target comes from.

What the AI may write on its own

  • Yes: technical remediation steps, filled templates, general explanations of the law with the article quoted, drafts for the lawyer.
  • Only with a lawyer's click in strict markets: anything that reasons about the company's specific facts, and any sentence that judges compliance.
  • Always: every generated sentence links to the article and the evidence it rests on. Ungrounded output is not shown. AI text is labelled as AI text.
4. What this means in the interfaceFour rules, one country setting

Two labels on every statement

Label 1

Prepared by Regulift

Facts, evidence, gap status, technical guidance, filled templates. Shown immediately, everywhere.

Label 2

Signed by Firm, 18 Oct 2026

Applicability, case-specific explanations, ratings, the confirmation. In closed markets these wait for the signature. In Germany and open markets more shows at once, with a "not legal advice" label.

Country setting

One setting per customer country decides which outputs need Label 2 before the customer sees them. Build the closed-market version first. Moving to Germany or Italy means switching features on, never off.

Citations and AI labels

Every sentence links to its article and its evidence item. AI-generated text and AI interaction are marked, which the AI Act requires since August 2026. A chatbot's answers are legally the company's own (OLG Hamm, May 2026), so gates matter, disclaimers do not.

5. Who pays whomPrices are our proposal within researched ranges

Three layers of revenue, none of them tied to legal fees

Platform Legal

€3,600 to €14,400 a year by company size (10 to 49 staff €3,600, 50 to 249 €7,200, 250 to 1,000 €14,400). Connectors, context report, inventory, evidence record and the GDPR checklist.

Regulation modules Legal

€1,800 to €6,000 per regulation a year by size: NIS2, AI Act, DORA for suppliers, CRA. A mid-sized company with three modules lands at €14,400 to €18,000. Per-framework pricing is the market norm (StartComply €4,500, Drata USD 7,500 per framework).

Partner law firms Legal

Flat yearly workspace fee, the same for every firm, priced for seats, storage and integrations. The level is not backed by any source yet and comes from the first partner talks. The firm bills the client directly for its legal work.

Never Critical

  • A fee per mandate or per lead (void in Germany since BGH IX ZR 89/23; banned in Austria, France, Czechia).
  • A percentage of the lawyer's fees.
  • Collecting or forwarding the client's legal fees.
  • A stake in a law firm or a share of its profit.
6. Which markets, in which orderAnd what changes at each border

Austria first, then Germany, then Italy. Budapest for pilots.

Austria, 2026 to 2027

  • NIS2 law live since 1 Oct 2026, registration due within three months: the freshest trigger in Europe.
  • One founder on the ground, Vienna under three hours from Budapest.
  • Expensive lawyers (about €349/h tariff), so the savings pitch lands.
  • Strictest rules: what passes here passes everywhere. Build the closed-market version here.

Germany, H2 2027

  • 80,043 firms with 50+ staff, ten times Austria.
  • Same language, same legal logic, plus the Smartlaw safe harbour: more shows without a signature.
  • New: a German partner firm and the German NIS2 rule set. Entry through firms, because the field is crowded.

Italy, 2028

  • 31,851 firms with 50+ staff and a native-speaking founder.
  • Open rules on advice; the firm must disclose its AI use (Law 132/2025).
  • Cheaper lawyers, so lead with readiness and speed. Start in the north.

Hungary, throughout

  • Pilot and engineering base, not a revenue market: legal spend a quarter of Austria's, criminal risk for unauthorised advice.
  • Its two-yearly compulsory NIS2 audit is a perfect test for the evidence pack.

What travels unchanged: the product, the evidence, the legal structure, GDPR, AI Act, DORA and CRA (one text everywhere). What is new per country: the national NIS2 rule set, the partner firm, the country setting for the labels, and the sales motion. The Netherlands is the best English-language fourth market; France has no NIS2 law yet; Switzerland is outside both NIS2 and the AI Act.

7. What to build firstThe Austrian version, in 90 days

Build the strict version, then unlock

Days 1 to 30

Prove the pain

15 discovery interviews in Austria and Budapest. Letter of intent from one Vienna law firm, which reviews this structure. Collector prototype: GitHub and one cloud provider, read-only, context report generated and approved.

Days 31 to 60

Prove the flow

Two design partners connect real systems. The firm's GDPR and NIS2 checklists are live with acceptance criteria. Gap view, technical guidance and the signature trail work end to end.

Days 61 to 90

Prove the value

First evidence pack confirmed in writing by the firm. Lawyer hours measured against the firm's own quote. Pricing tested with both partners. Company incorporated, aws Preseed application prepared.

Months 4 to 12

Scale the loop

HR and CRM connectors. AI Act module and templates. Ten paying pilots in Austria, second firm. German partner firm signed, German rule set, country setting switched to "strict with safe harbour".

MVP scope, in one list

  • Collector with MCP connectors for GitHub and one cloud, read-only scopes, context report, representative approval.
  • Inventory and evidence record with links to sources and continuous re-reading.
  • Firm checklist engine: GDPR, NIS2, AI Act items with acceptance criteria, authored and published under the firm's name.
  • Gap view and technical guidance, shown instantly. Templates filled from the inventory.
  • Lawyer workspace: drafts queue, sign and release, signature trail, "ask the lawyer".
  • Country setting that gates which outputs wait for a signature. Default: closed market.

Not in the MVP

  • Any output that judges the company without a lawyer's signature.
  • DORA, CRA and Data Act modules (next, in that order).
  • Billing between company and firm. The firm invoices directly.
  • A data protection officer service (possible later in Austria for non-lawyers, with legal tasks going to the firm).
8. Still openSettle these with the first partner firm

Six questions for an Austrian lawyer

Checklist pattern Open

Confirm that a gap view against a firm-authored checklist counts as mechanical comparison, not advice, given the Vienna ruling that treated even legal information as reserved.

Templates and explanations Open

Confirm the "firm's material delivered through Regulift" framing for filled templates and general explanations of the law.

Framework mandate Open

Draft the day-one engagement between company and firm that covers questions during the loop at a flat or capped fee.

Workspace fee level Open

No source gives a price. Set it with the first two firms, priced for seats, storage and integrations, identical for every firm.

Lawyers as investors Open

Whether partner lawyers may hold shares in Regulift was not researched. Ask the Austrian Bar before any round that includes lawyers.

AI tools and the bar Open

No Austrian court has ruled on AI in legal work. The risk is a bar complaint under unfair-competition law, not a criminal case. Keep the gates, keep the labels, keep the firm's name on the judgements.
